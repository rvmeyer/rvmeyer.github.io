I am currently a fifth-year PhD candidate at the University of Florida, where I am advised by Dr. Ratree Wayland. 

I am also a part of the <a href = "slam.lin.ufl.edu">Speech, Lexicon, and Modeling Lab</a>. My dissertation focuses on analyzing consonant clusters across dialectal Arabic through both a phonetic and computational lens. I was also a research assistant for the <a href = "https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award/?AWD_ID=2037266">SELMA project</a>, where I used neural network models to assess acoustic characteristics in language acquisition and clinical contexts.

I completed my bachelor's degree in linguistics and psychology at The Ohio State University, where I worked on second language acquisition and coarticulation.

My research interests are primarily in dialectal Arabic phonetics and phonology, second language acquisition of phonetics, and speech as a biomarker in clinical contexts.
