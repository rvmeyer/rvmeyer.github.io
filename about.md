I graduated with my PhD in Linguistics from the University of Florida in August 2026, where I was advised by <a href = "https://people.clas.ufl.edu/ratree/">Dr. Ratree Wayland</a>. My dissertation focused on analyzing consonant clusters across dialectal Arabic through both a phonetic and computational lens, including developing <a href = "github.com/rvmeyer/ar-forced-aligner">a multi-dialectal forced aligner</a> to aid the analysis.

I was also a part of the <a href = "slam.lin.ufl.edu">Speech, Lexicon, and Modeling Lab</a>. From 2023-2025, I was a research assistant for the <a href = "https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award/?AWD_ID=2037266">SELMA project</a>, where I used neural network models to assess acoustic characteristics in language acquisition and clinical contexts. In particular, we looked at lenition of voiced stop allophones as a measure of acquisition for <a href = "https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167639325001165">English speakers learning Spanish</a>, and as a measure of disease progression in English- and Spanish-speaking <a href = "https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/15/11/1162"> patients with Parkinson's disease</a> and <a href = "https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7426/4/4/123">similar neurodegenerative disorders</a>.

I completed my bachelor's degree in linguistics and psychology at The Ohio State University, where I worked on second language acquisition and coarticulation.

My research interests are primarily in dialectal Arabic phonetics and phonology, second language acquisition, and speech as a biomarker in clinical contexts. Additionally, I'm interested in developing speech technology for all three groups, which tend to fall outside high-resource norms.

In my free time, I enjoy reading, cross-stitching, and baking.
