I graduated with my PhD in Linguistics from the University of Florida in August 2026, where I was advised by Dr. Ratree Wayland.

I was also a part of the Speech, Lexicon, and Modeling Lab. My dissertation focused on analyzing consonant clusters across dialectal Arabic through both a phonetic and computational lens, and created a forced alignment system for dialectal Arabic. I was also a research assistant for the SELMA project, where I used neural network models to assess lenition of voiced stop allophones in two different speech contexts: as a measure of acquisition for English speakers learning Spanish, and as a measure of disease progression in English- and Spanish-speaking patients with Parkinson’s disease and similar neurodegenerative disorders.

I completed my bachelor's degree in Linguistics and Psychology at The Ohio State University, where I worked on second language acquisition and coarticulation.

My research interests are primarily in dialectal Arabic phonetics and phonology, second language acquisition, and speech as a biomarker in clinical contexts. Additionally, I'm interested in developing speech technology for all three groups, which tend to fall outside high-resource norms.
